# 📱🌍 Social Media Usage and Mental Health Analysis

## 🧠 Project Overview

While browsing social media reels, I came across a short video where a woman mentioned that the day she opens any social media platform, that day becomes mentally exhausting. While this statement may be subjective or exaggerated, it raises an important question about the psychological impact of social media platforms like **X (Twitter), Instagram, and YouTube**, which are often criticized for toxicity, algorithmic bias, and emotionally charged content distribution.

This project aims to analyze whether social media usage truly impacts mental fatigue and how different behavioral and platform-level factors contribute to it.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- To analyze whether higher social media usage increases mental fatigue
- To evaluate platform-wise mental fatigue differences
- To study relationship between engagement and mental fatigue
- To analyze demographic impact (age-based differences)
- To examine country-level digital overload
- To visualize global mental fatigue evolution over time

---

## 📊 Key Findings

### 1. Usage vs Mental Fatigue
A correlation analysis was performed between daily usage and mental fatigue:
Correlation = -0.004616353594368001

🔎 Insight:
- No significant linear relationship was found
- Mental fatigue depends more on **content type and context**, not usage duration

---

### 2. Platform-wise Mental Fatigue

- TikTok shows the highest mental fatigue levels
- Followed by Instagram and X (Twitter)
- YouTube shows comparatively lower fatigue

🔎 Insight:
Platform design and content structure significantly influence cognitive load.

---

### 3. Engagement vs Mental Fatigue

- Very weak correlation observed between engagement score and mental fatigue
- Indicates engagement alone is not a strong predictor of mental well-being

🔎 Insight:
Cognitive load and content nature matter more than interaction level.

---

### 4. Demographic Analysis

- Teens:
  - Avg usage: **184 minutes/day**
  - Mental fatigue: **5.61**
- Adults/Seniors:
  - Avg usage: **179 minutes/day**
  - Mental fatigue: **5.5**

🔎 Insight:
Younger users show slightly higher fatigue, but differences are not extreme.

---

### 5. Country-level Digital Overload

Countries like:
- United Kingdom
- Pakistan
- India
- Canada

show higher digital overload patterns.

🔎 Insight:
Differences may be influenced by:
- Content type exposure
- Platform usage distribution
- Algorithmic content targeting
- Paid vs organic content bias

---

### 6. Platform Influence on Fatigue

- Instagram shows strong influence on mental fatigue
- YouTube follows with moderate impact

🔎 Insight:
Visual-heavy platforms tend to contribute more to cognitive strain.

---

### 7. Temporal & Global Trends

- Animated analysis shows increasing fatigue over time
- TikTok exhibits highest concentration of high-fatigue regions
- X shows mixed distribution patterns globally

---

## 🧠 Conclusion

This study highlights that:

- Social media usage duration alone does NOT determine mental fatigue
- Content type, platform design, and algorithmic exposure play a larger role
- Mental fatigue is a multi-factor behavioral phenomenon rather than a linear metric

However, the findings also suggest:

- Social media platforms may contribute to psychological strain in certain contexts
- Content moderation and algorithm transparency remain important areas of concern
- Further research is needed to separate organic vs algorithm-driven content effects

---

## ⚠️ Final Note

This project does not claim causation. Instead, it provides a **data-driven observational analysis** of behavioral patterns across platforms and demographics.
